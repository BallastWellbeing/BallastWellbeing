covers: >-
  The core insight is that a dysregulated nervous system produces behaviour that
  looks exactly like defiance, and that the response which works for one makes
  the other worse. Staff learn to read the difference in the first thirty seconds
  of an incident, then practise a first response that does not depend on knowing
  a student's history. The full day adds practice labs using incidents the staff
  bring from their own classrooms.
outcomes:
  - "Distinguish a dysregulated student from a defiant one using observable signals, not background assumptions"
  - "Deliver a first response that lowers arousal rather than escalating it, practised aloud"
  - "Explain to a colleague why a consequence delivered mid-escalation does not land"
  - "Identify the predictable flashpoints in their own timetable and change one of them"
  - "Decide when a behaviour is theirs to manage and when it belongs in a referral"
  - "Apply the school's referral protocol without waiting for permission in the moment"

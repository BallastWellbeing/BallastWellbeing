covers: >-
  The conversation most managers avoid is the one where an employee is visibly
  struggling and nobody has named it. Managers practise a short, goal-directed
  structure that requires no diagnosis and no clinical knowledge, which is
  deliberate: a manager's job is to notice, respond, and refer, not to assess.
  The session spends most of its time on rehearsal rather than theory, because
  this fails in execution rather than in understanding.
outcomes:
  - "Open the conversation with an observation rather than a question, and know why that works better"
  - "Run a short goal-directed exchange without asking for a diagnosis or a history"
  - "Respond to a disclosure they were not expecting without freezing or over-promising"
  - "State clearly what they can and cannot keep confidential, before the disclosure rather than after"
  - "Close with one agreed next step and a date, and follow it up"
  - "Refer to the organisation's own supports accurately, having checked what they actually cover"

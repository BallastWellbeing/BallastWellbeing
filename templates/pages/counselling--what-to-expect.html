What to expect

Before the first session

I'll send you an intake form and a written consent agreement. The consent form covers what you'll have read on the main page — confidentiality and its limits, supervision, fees, cancellation, and how your record is kept. Read it properly and ask about anything that isn't clear. You're consenting to treatment, and that should be an informed decision rather than a formality.

The first session

The first fifteen minutes are administrative — consent, questions, how this works. Then we talk about what brought you.

I'll ask what you want to be different. Not what's wrong with you — what you want to be different. Some people know immediately. Most don't, and working that out is part of the work.

You don't have to tell me your whole history in the first session. You can tell me as much or as little as you want, in whatever order makes sense to you.

After that

Sessions are usually weekly at first. How long people stay varies — some come for six sessions with a specific goal, some longer. We'll check in periodically about whether this is useful. If it isn't, I'd rather know, and I'd rather help you find someone who fits better than keep going out of politeness.

Telephone sessions

Most of my practice is by phone. Find somewhere private where you won't be interrupted, and where you can talk freely. A parked car works better than you'd think.

Your record

I keep clinical notes as required by the College. They're stored securely and retained for the period the College requires. You can request access to your record at any time. The clinical privacy notice sets out how that works.

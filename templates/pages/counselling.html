What "Qualifying" means

Registered Psychotherapist (Qualifying) is a registration category of the College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario. It means I am registered with the College, bound by its standards, and practising under the supervision of an experienced clinician while completing the College's final registration requirements.

In practice this means your care is reviewed. My clinical work is discussed regularly with my supervisor, which is an additional layer of oversight rather than a lesser standard of care. What that means for your privacy is set out below.

Who I work with

I work with adults, university and graduate students, new graduates, young people moving into post-secondary life, and older adults.

People usually come to me about anxiety, trauma and post-traumatic stress, life transitions, relationship difficulties, work stress and burnout, and periods where something has shifted and the usual ways of coping have stopped working.

How I work

Solution-Focused Brief Therapy was my graduate specialisation and is where most of my work starts. It's practical and directed at what you want to be different, rather than an open-ended exploration of history. For some people that's enough on its own.

Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioural Therapy informs the work when trauma or post-traumatic stress is central.

Gottman Method research shapes how I work with relationship and conflict difficulties.

Most of my practice is by telephone. That isn't a compromise — for a lot of people it's easier to say difficult things without a face on a screen, and it removes travel, parking, and waiting rooms from a day that's already full.

What stays private, and what can't

What you tell me is confidential and your record is protected under Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act.

There are limits, and you should know them before we begin:

  • Clinical supervision. As a qualifying registrant, I discuss my clinical work with my supervisor. Your identifying information is minimised, but your care is discussed.
  • Risk of serious harm. If I believe there is a risk of serious harm to you or someone else, I may have to act on it.
  • Child protection. I am legally required to report a suspicion that a child under 16 is or may be in need of protection.
  • Sexual abuse by a health professional. I am required to report this to the relevant College.
  • Court order. A record can be ordered by a court.

Outside these, nothing leaves the room without your written consent.
